# 3. Challenge Mission

The challenge is a global competition focused on developing intelligent, trustworthy, sustainable technical solutions that serve digital Islamic content and support civilizational communication and the presentation of Islam internationally.

The official materials frame the challenge around real problems in digital Islamic content, including issues such as:

- reliability and scientific/religious integrity;
- hallucination and distortion risks;
- scarcity or fragmentation of documented data;
- weak or missing technical infrastructure;
- multilingual and cross-cultural communication;
- access to trustworthy knowledge;
- content verification and evidence tracing;
- practical deployment and sustainability.

Teams compete to develop AI-enabled solutions addressing real challenges while preserving reliability, user experience, practical applicability, and sustainability.

---

# 4. Participation Scope

Participation is global and remote, without a geographic restriction stated by the official challenge materials.

A participant may enter:

- individually; or
- as a team of **2 to 5 members**, including the team leader.

Each person may have one participation and one idea/project submission under the stated rules.

The guide recommends, but does not require, a multidisciplinary team that can cover areas such as:

- AI / model development;
- interfaces or application development;
- Islamic or knowledge-domain review;
- user-experience design;
- content and testing responsibilities.

Participation is not restricted to one academic or professional specialization.

# 5. Target Participant Groups

The official materials identify broad target groups including:

- developers and technical builders;
- AI engineers and data scientists;
- natural-language-processing and computer-vision specialists;
- Islamic researchers and subject-matter specialists;
- linguists and content specialists;
- reviewers / verifiers and content creators;
- user-experience designers and product managers;
- entrepreneurs and startup teams;
- universities and students;
- nonprofit / third-sector institutions;
- other interested innovators.

These are **participant groups**, not automatically the end users of a project.

Every proposed project still needs a clearly defined beneficiary or user population.

# 6. Challenge Tracks

A project should select the primary track that best matches its problem and beneficiary.

The tracks are not generic technology categories. Each track contains an intended user benefit and an explicit project-success question.

## Track 1 — Knowledge Dialogue and Trustworthy Answers

**Arabic:** الحوار المعرفي والإجابات الموثوقة

### Official intent

Develop conversational solutions that provide answers that are correct, clear, documented, and appropriate to the questioner's context and background, while relying on approved sources and referring to a qualified specialist when needed or when a trustworthy answer cannot be provided.

### Official success criterion

The project should demonstrate whether the solution can:

- provide a correct and clear answer suitable for the questioner;
- trace the answer to an approved source;
- refuse to answer or refer to a specialist when sufficient reliable reference support is unavailable.

---

## Track 2 — Multilingual Content and Cultural Localization

**Arabic:** صناعة المحتوى متعدد اللغات والتوطين الثقافي

### Official intent

Develop solutions that transfer and adapt Islamic content across languages and cultures accurately and faithfully while preserving religious meaning, terminology accuracy, and suitability of communication for the target cultural context.

### Official success criterion

The project should demonstrate whether the solution preserves:

- intended Islamic meaning;
- accuracy of religious terminology;
- the essence of the source content;

while improving clarity and cultural suitability for the target audience.

---

## Track 3 — Interactive Experiences and Knowledge Journeys for Learning About Islam

**Arabic:** التجارب التفاعلية والرحلة المعرفية للتعريف بالإسلام وتعلمه

### Official intent

Create interactive knowledge experiences that support understanding, exploration, participation, and learning through contemporary methods beyond passive information delivery.

Solutions may adapt content and services to the beneficiary's need and stage of knowledge and may support a gradual journey from initial interest through learning and continued engagement, with human or specialist referral when needed.

### Official success criterion

The project should demonstrate measurable improvement in areas such as:

- understanding an Islamic concept;
- appropriateness of content for the target user;
- continuity and logical progression of the learning journey.

The guide also explicitly emphasizes privacy and warns against inferring or classifying sensitive or religious attributes about users without a legitimate project basis.

---

## Track 4 — Knowledge and Verification Tools for People Who Introduce Islam

**Arabic:** أدوات المعرفة والتحقق لتمكين المعرفين بالإسلام

### Official intent

Develop tools for researchers, specialists, and people working in the field of introducing Islam that improve rapid access to trustworthy knowledge, verification of content and sources, and evaluation of the strength of associated evidence.

### Official success criterion

The project should demonstrate whether it improves the accuracy of knowledge access or verification while making the following visible and traceable:

- the source;
- the status of evidence;
- what the available sources actually support;
- what requires further verification or specialist referral.

---

## Track 5 — Open Track: Innovative Solutions for Introducing Islam

**Arabic:** المسار المفتوح: حلول مبتكرة في التعريف بالإسلام

### Official intent

Accommodate innovative AI-enabled solutions supporting civilizational communication and the introduction of Islam that either:

- do not fall directly within Tracks 1–4; or
- combine more than one track in a single coherent solution.

### Official success criterion

The team should identify:

- a real and specific problem;
- a specific target audience;
- a working solution;
- a measurable effect / impact indicator;
- a reason the project is not simply duplicating one of the first four tracks.

The open track is not an exemption from the challenge's common reliability, evidence, usability, and execution requirements.

# 7. Technology Guidance

The Participant Guide lists example technologies such as:

- natural-language processing;
- generative AI;
- retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and source-linked responses;
- knowledge graphs and network analysis;
- speech AI and speech-to-text / text-to-speech;
- computer vision and OCR;
- sentiment / pattern analysis and indicator extraction.

These technologies are **illustrative, not mandatory**.

The guide states that technology should be selected according to the problem and function of the solution, and that AI is evaluated by its demonstrated value rather than by technical complexity alone.

Therefore this challenge brief must never be interpreted as requiring an LLM, RAG, knowledge graph, voice system, or any other particular architecture.

# 9. Registration / Selection Criteria

The official Participant Guide gives the following weights for nomination and acceptance:

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Problem clarity and fit with the track and audience | 25% |
| Reliability and scientific/safety plan | 20% |
| Appropriateness of AI use and the value it adds | 15% |
| Feasibility during the challenge period | 15% |
| Originality and added value | 15% |
| Ability to execute and coverage of required tasks | 10% |

## What the guide expects behind these criteria

### Problem clarity and track/audience fit

Strong submissions identify:

- a specific problem;
- a clear user / beneficiary;
- a real use case;
- evidence or a documented description of the current practice;
- the intended improvement;
- a testable success criterion.

### AI appropriateness and added value

The submission should explain:

- the actual task AI performs;
- why that capability is appropriate to the problem;
- what improves compared with the current method or a simpler alternative;
- limitations and expected failure handling;
- how AI's added value could be tested.

### Reliability and scientific/safety plan

The submission should identify appropriate sources and verification behavior, including where relevant:

- source attribution;
- content accuracy;
- terminology accuracy;
- refusal or escalation behavior;
- human review;
- expected errors and how they will be handled;
- test cases involving conflicting, missing, or insufficient reference support.

### Feasibility

The proposal should have a scope that can realistically produce and test its core working function during the three-day challenge period using available resources.

### Originality and added value

The challenge does not reward cosmetic difference alone. The guide expects a meaningful improvement compared with a known alternative or current practice, preferably with a testable way to demonstrate that improvement.

### Execution capability

The individual or team should reasonably cover the technical, content, and testing tasks required by the proposed scope, or present a credible plan for covering them.

# 11. Expected Final Deliverables

The official guide requires a complete project package rather than only an idea or visual prototype.

Expected outputs include:

1. **A complete working digital solution** ready for actual operation/use.
2. **A public GitHub repository** containing the code and publishable project files with operating/setup documentation and no secrets or sensitive user data.
3. **A demonstration video no longer than 2 minutes.**
4. **A presentation in PDF or PowerPoint format** covering the problem, solution, workflow, added value, technologies, results, and continuation plan.
5. **Documentation of content, Islamic/knowledge sources, tools, dependencies, and licensing / rights information.**
6. **A working Live Demo link** available for judges to test and review.

The final solution must not be only a prototype.

The guide explicitly states that the project should work as a complete product and demonstrate realistic applicability and actual use.

Presentation materials may be in Arabic or English according to the guide.

# 12. GitHub and Documentation Requirements

The repository submitted for judging must be **public**; a private repository is not accepted by the guide.

The repository should include the complete code the team has the right to publish and should document:

- setup;
- operation;
- dependencies;
- sources;
- tools;
- licenses / rights;
- relevant project documentation.

Do not publish:

- passwords;
- API keys;
- secrets;
- private user data;
- other sensitive material.

# 13. Use of Existing Projects

The official FAQ permits use of a project that existed before the challenge provided that the team:

- documents the starting/baseline version;
- discloses relevant rights;
- clearly distinguishes prior work from new work.

The guide states that evaluation focuses on what is completed during the challenge period **4–6 October 2026**.

Therefore existing work must not be presented as though it was built entirely during the challenge.

# 14. Judging Process

Judging occurs remotely in two stages.

## Stage 1 — Platform Review

All eligible submitted projects are evaluated through the platform using the required files, working solution link, GitHub repository, and product-evaluation criteria.

Up to **20 projects** may be nominated for final judging. The guide notes that the full number does not have to be filled if sufficient qualifying projects do not exist.

## Stage 2 — Final Judging via Zoom

Finalists present the submitted product itself, with operation and verification available to the judging committee.

The nomination/acceptance score from the earlier stage is not added to the final result.

---

# 15. Final Judging Session

Each finalist receives:

- **5 minutes** for presentation;
- **3 minutes** for questions;
- **8 minutes total** per team.

The judging committee may select up to **5 winning projects** that meet the winning and eligibility threshold.

---

# 16. Final Judging Criteria

The official final-stage criteria are:

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Technical solution quality and AI use | 25% |
| Benefit achieved according to the track success criterion | 20% |
| Reliability and scientific/safety integrity | 15% |
| Innovation and added value | 15% |
| Beneficiary experience, communication, and accessibility | 10% |
| Operational realism and completeness | 10% |
| Presentation clarity and verifiability | 5% |

## What the final rubric emphasizes

### Technical solution quality and AI use

Judges assess whether the product works reliably, whether AI performs a real function with a clear methodology, whether it handles varied cases within scope, and whether its limitations are documented.

### Reliability and scientific/safety integrity

The rubric examines content and source integrity, attribution, refusal/escalation where required, handling of conflict or insufficient information, traceability, human review where appropriate, and transparency about limits and errors.

### Innovation and added value

The strongest evidence of innovation is not novelty in appearance but demonstrated improvement compared with a specific alternative or current practice.

### User experience, communication, and accessibility

The target beneficiary should be able to complete the intended task and understand outputs through clear, respectful communication appropriate to background and language. Relevant accessibility needs should be considered and tested.

### Benefit according to track success criterion

The project should demonstrate measurable improvement on the selected track's own success criterion rather than only make theoretical claims of benefit.

### Operational realism and completeness

Judges consider what is required to keep the solution operating after the challenge, including dependencies, cost, content maintenance/review, responsibilities, and realistic next steps.

### Presentation clarity and verifiability

The presentation should make the problem, solution, evidence, result, and limitations understandable and should make important claims reasonably verifiable or reproducible.

# 19. Important Non-Assumptions

Agents must not infer the following merely from the challenge theme:

- that every valid problem requires a chatbot;
- that every project requires a large language model;
- that RAG is mandatory;
- that a more complex AI system scores better;
- that an Islamic AI system should answer every question;
- that a missing feature proves a meaningful user problem;
- that technical novelty substitutes for measurable benefit;
- that generic "Muslim users" is a sufficiently specific audience;
- that a Figma prototype alone satisfies final submission requirements;
- that an existing project may be submitted without disclosing its baseline;
- that a source-backed response is automatically correct without checking source fit, context, and claim support.
