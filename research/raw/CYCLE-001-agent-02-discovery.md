## 1. Research Scope
This research investigates the real-world experiences, cognitive barriers, information-seeking workflows, and friction points encountered by non-Arabic speaking individuals discovering, exploring, or newly practicing Islam.

The cohort specifically investigated encompasses:
1. **Curious Explorers & Serious Seekers:** Non-Muslims exploring Islamic theology, ethics, and practices, seeking objective clarity while navigating public misinformation and foreign vocabulary.
2. **Newly Converted Muslims (Reverts) — First 90 Days:** Individuals who have recently taken the *Shahada*, facing immediate, high-stakes physical worship obligations (Salah, Taharah) without foundational muscle memory or Arabic literacy.
3. **Early-Stage Practicing Converts — Months 3 to 12:** Converts navigating daily-life ethical, dietary, social, and familial dilemmas in non-Muslim environments, encountering conflicting legal rulings (*Fatwas*) and community integration friction.

## 3. User Stages
Rather than treating "people interested in Islam" as a monolith, research establishes five distinct epistemic and behavioural stages:

| Stage ID | Stage Name | Defining Goal | Dominant Information Need | Primary Failure Mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Stage A** | Curious Explorer | Understand what Islam teaches objectively | Neutral, accessible conceptual overviews; debunking media stereotypes | Encountering dense sectarian polemics or hostile apologetics |
| **Stage B** | Serious Seeker | Compare beliefs; evaluate theological claims | Comparative theology (Jesus, Bible vs Quran, Monotheism vs Trinity) | Untranslated Arabic theological jargon; aggressive proselytization pressure |
| **Stage C** | Pre-Conversion Learner | Clarify practical requirements before conversion | "What will my life look like?"; "How do I become Muslim?" | Fearing immediate lifestyle perfection is required before declaring faith |
| **Stage D** | Newly Converted Muslim (Days 1–90) | Learn and execute daily worship (Salah, Wudu) | Step-by-step motor-phonetic prayer guidance; structured gradualism (*Tadrij*) | Severe cognitive overload, prayer anxiety, floor cheat-sheet friction |
| **Stage E** | Early Learner (Months 3–12+) | Navigate family, social ethics, halal diet, community | Contextual fiqh; understanding legitimate scholarly disagreement (*Ikhtilaf*) | "Fatwa whiplash", family estrangement, burnout from "haram police" |

## 4. Discovery Journey
The pre-conversion journey from initial interest to commitment follows an observable multi-step pathway:

```text
[1. Spark / Trigger] ──> [2. Search & Media] ──> [3. First Reading] ──> [4. Deep Questions] ──> [5. Mosque / Human Contact] ──> [6. Shahada]
      │                         │                       │                      │                         │                           │
  Life crisis,             YouTube, Reddit,       Introductory web        Theological doubts,       First visit to local        Declaration of faith
  Muslim peer,             TikTok, podcasts       articles, Qur'an       morality, lifestyle       masjid; seeking imam        at mosque or alone
  news event                                      translation             fears, family impact      or convert mentor           in private
```

**Key Breakdown Points Identified in Discovery:**
- **At Step 3 (First Reading):** Seekers hit walls of undefined Arabic technical vocabulary (*Taqwa*, *Ihsan*, *Sunnah*, *Bid'ah*). English translations often distort the meaning (e.g., *Bid'ah* translated simply as "innovation").
- **At Step 5 (Mosque Contact):** Many seekers experience intense hesitation due to language/cultural barriers (many Western mosques operate primarily in Urdu, Somali, or Arabic, with limited English introductory material and segregated or inaccessible women's spaces).

## 5. Post-Conversion Learning Journey
The post-conversion experience is non-linear and carries a massive cognitive burden:

- **Day 1 to Week 1:** Immense initial emotional high ("honeymoon phase") followed by immediate shock: realizing there are 5 daily obligatory prayers with 17 rak'ahs in Arabic, plus intricate purification rites (*Ghusl*, *Wudu*).
- **Week 2 to Month 3:** The "Solitary Struggle." Convert tries to pray at home using makeshift cheat sheets, stumbling through foreign phonetics, battling obsessive doubts (*Waswas*) over prayer validity.
- **Month 3 to Month 12:** The "Fiqh & Social Maze." First major holidays arrive (Thanksgiving, Christmas, Easter, family birthdays). Convert searches online for guidance, encounters strict fatwas declaring holiday presence "haram", enters painful disputes with non-Muslim parents, and experiences "fatwa whiplash".

## 6. Major Workflow Friction

### Workflow A: Performing Solitary Obligatory Prayer (Salah)
- **Step 1 (Preparation):** Convert checks prayer time app; performs Wudu while double-checking steps on an infographic.
- **Step 2 (Setup):** Convert places a printed transliteration cheat sheet or smartphone playing a tutorial on the floor at the front edge of the prayer mat.
- **Step 3 (Execution):** Convert stands, bows, and prostrates while squinting at the floor text, attempting to recite phonetically unfamiliar Arabic (*Ghayril maghdoobi 'alayhim...*) while synchronizing physical movements.
- **Step 4 (Interruption):** Convert stumbles over a word, loses their place on the paper, forgets whether they are on the 2nd or 3rd rak'ah, or the smartphone screen locks mid-prostration.
- **Outcome:** Convert finishes prayer in a state of high anxiety and guilt, questioning whether their prayer was completely invalid.

### Workflow B: Navigating Everyday Family & Dietary Dilemmas
- **Step 1 (Dilemma):** Non-Muslim parents invite convert to Thanksgiving/Christmas family dinner.
- **Step 2 (Unguided Search):** Convert Googles: *"Can new Muslim attend family Christmas dinner?"*
- **Step 3 (Strict Fatwa Encounter):** Top search result (IslamQA.info) declares attendance strictly prohibited, citing imitation of disbelievers and sitting near alcohol.
- **Step 4 (Conflicting Advice Encounter):** Troubled, convert asks on Reddit `r/converts` or consults SeekersGuidance; receives opposite advice based on Western fiqh councils and filial piety (*Birr al-Walidayn*).
- **Outcome:** Convert experiences "fatwa whiplash" and moral paralysis, feeling forced to choose between abandoning Islamic law or cutting off their non-Muslim parents.

## 7. Terminology and Translation Findings
- **The Loanword Barrier:** English Islamic resources frequently use 30+ untranslated Arabic loanwords per article without contextual definitions or tooltips.
- **The "False Equivalence" Translation Trap:**
  - *Sunnah* translated as "tradition" (misses juristic distinction between recommended acts and normative prophetic practice).
  - *Bid'ah* translated as "innovation" (causes converts to assume technological tools or speaking English in daily life are forbidden heresies).
  - *Taqwa* translated as "fear of God" (misses the core meaning of active mindfulness, reverence, and spiritual protection).
- **Transliteration Inconsistency:** A single concept is spelled in multiple divergent ways (*Salah / Salat / Salaat / Namaz*; *Wudu / Wudhu / Wudhoo*; *Zikr / Dhikr / Thikr*), breaking search queries for novices attempting to research concepts heard verbally.

---

## 8. Trust and Source Findings
- **SEO Distortion:** Literalistic and hyper-stringent fatwa repositories (specifically IslamQA.info) dominate search engine rankings for beginner English queries.
- **Lack of *Ikhtilaf* Literacy:** Beginners possess no mental model of the four canonical Sunni schools of thought (*Madhahib*) or legitimate juristic diversity. When they see two scholars give different rulings, they conclude that one scholar is "corrupt" or that Islam is contradictory.
- **The "Haram Police" Phenomenon:** Unmoderated online spaces (TikTok comments, Reddit, Facebook groups) subject converts to aggressive policing by anonymous peers who present personal or cultural stringencies as absolute Islamic dogma.

---

## 9. Community and Human-Support Findings
- **"Double Othering":** As documented in the Cambridge University convert studies (Prof. Yasir Suleiman, 2013/2016), converts are caught between two worlds: alienated by non-Muslim family and society, while viewed as "perpetual novices" or "cultural outsiders" in immigrant-majority mosques.
- **Cultural Conflation:** Heritage Muslims frequently impose ethnic customs (South Asian, Arab, or Somali dress, dietary preferences, domestic customs) as though they were divine law. Converts who resist adopting immigrant cultural identities are accused of lacking religious sincerity.
- **Mosque Language Isolation:** Many Western masjids operate administrative and social affairs in heritage languages (Urdu, Arabic, Bengali, Somali), lacking structured newcomer onboarding or English-language convert support halaqahs.

## 10. Strongest Problem Patterns
Through extensive triangulation of evidence, three core high-friction problems emerged as the strongest, most urgent candidates:

1. **[P-100] Post-Shahada Salah Learning Overload & Motor-Recitation Anxiety (Gradualism Deficit)**
   - *Core Pain:* Immediate imposition of full adult Arabic recitation without structured pedagogical gradualism (*Tadrij*), causing severe performance anxiety and prayer avoidance.
2. **[P-101] Convert "Fatwa Whiplash" & Conflicting Guidance Paralysis (Ikhtilaf & Madhhab Illiteracy)**
   - *Core Pain:* Unguided search exposure to diametrically opposed legal verdicts on daily-life family/dietary dilemmas without methodological context or madhhab literacy, leading to family fractures and moral anxiety.
3. **[P-102] Untranslated Sacred Terminology & Cultural-Religious Conflation Barrier for Independent Seekers & Converts**
   - *Core Pain:* Educational materials saturated with undefined Arabic technical jargon and regional ethnic customs conflated with divine law, causing comprehension breakdown and cultural imposter syndrome.

## 12. Existing Solutions and Reference Projects
Initial lightweight Reference Candidates identified during Phase 1 discovery (full canonical reference teardowns reserved for Agent 05):

- **Namaz App (Mobile):** Step-by-step 2D visual guide with audio playback. *Limitations:* Static, Hanafi-only without contextual explanation, requires touching screen with wet or folded hands during prayer.
- **Step by Step Salat (Mobile):** Text and audio walkthrough. *Limitations:* Dense walls of text, rigid speed, intrusive commercial advertisements that shatter spiritual concentration.
- **My Salah Mat / Tenfold Interactive Mat (Hardware):** Physical interactive mat with touch sensors. *Limitations:* Bulky, expensive ($70–$100), fixed robot/child audio pacing, socially unusable when traveling or at work.
- **IslamQA.info (Web):** Massive fatwa archive. *Limitations:* Highly literalist Hanbali/Athari positions, zero contextual adaptation for Western convert family realities, triggers severe panic among novices.

- **Yaqeen Institute (Web/App):** Academic research publications addressing doubts. *Limitations:* Long-form papers (20+ pages) unsuitable for immediate, practical daily-life answers.

## 13. Disconfirming Evidence
Rigorous attempts were made to falsify the candidate problems:

- **Against P-100 (Salah Learning):** Many highly motivated converts report mastering basic recitations within 2 to 4 weeks through intense repetition. Furthermore, converts who learn inside an active, welcoming mosque can pray behind an Imam in congregation where individual recitation is waived. *Assessment:* While true for those with local mosque access, the problem remains severe and durable for the vast majority of geographically or socially isolated converts praying alone at home.
- **Against P-101 (Fatwa Whiplash):** Converts paired early with experienced, culturally competent mentors (e.g., Taleef Collective, Embrace) report minimal fatwa anxiety, as they are taught to follow one trusted local teacher and avoid internet queries. *Assessment:* Demonstrates that the problem is not inherent to Islamic jurisprudence, but is a digital discovery and mediation failure for the unmentored majority.
- **Against P-102 (Terminology Barrier):** Converts often take pride in acquiring Arabic vocabulary, viewing it as a sacred bond with the global Ummah, and actively resist "over-anglicizing" sacred terms like *Salah* or *Taqwa*. *Assessment:* The issue is not the presence of Arabic, but the lack of inline contextual definitions and the conflation of ethnic customs with religious law.

## 14. Rejected Hypotheses
In strict accordance with `AGENTS.md` Section 7:

1. **REJECTED HYPOTHESIS: "70% to 75% of converts leave Islam within a few years."**
   - *Investigation:* Thorough audit of demographic literature reveals this viral claim originates from an unpublished, informal 1996 telephone inquiry by Dr. Ilyas Ba-Yunus. Pew Research Center's empirical survey (2017) demonstrates that religious switching into Islam is roughly offset by switching out of Islam (a net stable population). The "75% exodus" is an ungrounded urban myth.
   - *Status:* **REJECTED & DISCONFIRMED** (documented in `E02-005`).
2. **REJECTED HYPOTHESIS: "Converts want all Arabic terminology completely translated into English."**
   - *Investigation:* User feedback shows converts value learning Arabic terms for worship and communal identity.
   - *Status:* **REJECTED.** Users do not want Arabic removed; they need seamless inline contextual definitions.
3. **REJECTED HYPOTHESIS: "New Muslims struggle primarily because articles are too long."**
   - *Investigation:* Evidence reveals that source trust, conflicting verdicts, and undefined jargon cause far more abandonment than text length alone.
   - *Status:* **DOWNGRADED.**

## 15. Islamic Reliability / Safety Constraints
All three problem domains involve sacred Islamic boundaries governed by `source-policy.md`:

- **Madhhab Neutrality & Preservation of *Ikhtilaf*:** Legitimate historical differences across orthodox jurisprudence (Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, Hanbali) must be represented transparently without sectarian polemics.

## 19. Overall Findings & Executive Summary
New Muslims and serious seekers face acute friction not from an absence of Islamic information, but from a severe **mediation, sequencing, and contextualization deficit**:

1. **Worship Practice:** Prayer apps demand immediate adult Arabic fluency and posture synchrony from Day 1, ignoring classical Prophetic gradualism (*Tadrij*), forcing converts into awkward floor-cheat-sheet workarounds and causing severe performance anxiety.
2. **Ethical Navigation:** Search engines expose novices to rigid, decontextualized legal verdicts (*Fatwa Whiplash*), causing unnecessary family rupture and moral neurosis due to complete illiteracy in legitimate juristic diversity (*Ikhtilaf*).
3. **Conceptual Learning:** Introductory content is saturated with undefined Arabic jargon and ethnocultural immigrant customs conflated with divine law, alienating seekers and inducing cultural imposter syndrome.

Any future technological intervention must earn its place by serving as a **contextual, gradualist, and transparent bridge**—grounded in qualified human scholarship, respectful of legitimate legal diversity, and rigorously safeguarded against autonomous religious edicts.
