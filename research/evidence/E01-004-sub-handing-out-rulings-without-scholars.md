# Evidence Record: E01-004

- **EVIDENCE ID:** E01-004
- **CLAIM SUPPORTED:** When practicing Muslims cannot resolve conflicting rulings via online search engines, they turn to community forums where unqualified users hand out absolutist rulings without scholarly attribution or treat single opinions as universal consensus (*Ijma'*), exacerbating sectarian polarization and user confusion.
- **SOURCE TYPE:** Public Forum Discussion (Reddit r/islam)
- **SOURCE:** Reddit thread on religious rulings on r/islam
- **URL:** https://www.reddit.com/r/islam/comments/54wxw3/do_you_think_this_sub_has_a_problem_with_handing/
- **DATE:** 2016-09-28
- **TARGET USER / POPULATION:** Practicing Muslims seeking reliable answers to practical religious questions
- **GEOGRAPHY:** Global
- **LANGUAGE:** English
- **DIRECT OR INDIRECT:** Direct observation of forum dynamics and user failure modes
- **PRIMARY OR SECONDARY:** Primary forum thread
- **KEY EVIDENCE:**
  > "I'm just wondering, because it seems like a lot of Muslims come to this sub looking for rulings, and the answers usually imply 'THIS is the correct way to do it'. A lot of issues in Islam have different, conflicting fatwas, and answers to questions don't always reflect that. I don't know how you're supposed to choose between fatwas in Islam, but shouldn't it come up more? I almost never see people say 'well, I looked it up, and found one fatwa that says this and one that says this. You should come to your own conclusion about which one to follow using [method you're supposed to use].'"
- **LIMITATIONS:** Thread observed in an online forum setting, but forum usage is a direct workaround for the failure of digital search to provide contextualized, balanced rulings.
- **CONTRADICTING EVIDENCE:** Some commenters argue that laypeople should follow one scholar or local imam blindly to avoid confusion.
- **STRENGTH:** Moderate
- **CONFIDENCE IN INTERPRETATION:** High
