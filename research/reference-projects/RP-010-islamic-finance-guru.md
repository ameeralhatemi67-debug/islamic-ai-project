- **Primary Language(s):** English.

---

## 3. Target User & Use Case
- **Target User:** Muslim working professionals, university graduates, and investors navigating modern economic life (crypto, pensions, mortgages, stock screening, student loans, wills).
- **Core Job:** "When I want to invest in a financial instrument or make a major financial decision, I want a clear breakdown of the Islamic legal debate, the fatwas for and against, the scholars behind each view, and a practical conclusion."

---

## 4. Key Capabilities & What It Solves
1. **Comparative Fatwa Architecture:** Unlike traditional fatwa sites, IFG explicitly models *Ikhtilaf*. For topics like Bitcoin, pensions, or student loans, IFG writes:
   - The Case for Permissibility (scholars, evidences, rationale).
   - The Case for Impermissibility (scholars, evidences, rationale).
   - Summary Table comparing views.
   - IFG's Editorial Take / Practical Verdict (*Muhtar*).
2. **Plain English Legal Scaffolding:** Eliminates dense Arabic jargon; explains financial concepts (*Gharar*, *Riba*, *Maysir*) using modern commercial legal analogies.
3. **High User Trust:** Founded by individuals holding both traditional Islamic Alimiyyah credentials and Western corporate law backgrounds.

---

## 5. Known Limitations & Why Our Problem Survives
1. **Domain Restricted to Finance:** IFG is exclusively a personal finance and investment platform. It does not cover daily worship, prayer doubts, convert family dilemmas, or medical ethics (`P-002`, `P-101`).
2. **High Human Editorial Overhead:** Every article requires weeks of manual legal analysis by human commercial lawyers. It is not an automated or scalable software engine.
3. **No Direct Fiqh Question-Answering Engine:** Operates as an editorial publication rather than a dynamic Q&A or search system.

---

## 6. Transferable Architectural Lessons for the Research Council
- IFG provides the **exact UX proof-of-concept** for how *Ikhtilaf* should be presented to lay Muslims: structured comparative columns, explicit naming of scholars/councils on each side, breakdown of underlying rationale (*Sabab al-Ikhtilaf*), and practical synthesis, proving that structured comparison *reduces* user anxiety rather than increasing it.
