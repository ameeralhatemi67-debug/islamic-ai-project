## 1. One-Sentence Problem Statement
Mosque imams, volunteer khatibs, and halaqah leaders struggle to verify the authenticity (*takhrij*), exact classical grading, and contextual circumstance (*Sabab al-Wurud*) of hadith narrations and historical anecdotes during weekly sermon preparation under intense time pressure, resulting in 4 to 8 hours of fragmented cross-referencing or the public propagation of weak (*da'if*) and fabricated (*mawdu'*) narrations from the pulpit.

## 2. Primary User & Context
- **Primary User:** Mosque Imams, Volunteer Khatibs (Friday sermon deliverers), Halaqah Instructors, and Public Islamic Lecturers.
  - *Experience Level:* Ranges from informally trained community volunteers (who hold secular full-time jobs) to classically trained seminary graduates (*Alimiyyah* holders) serving bilingual diaspora congregations.
  - *Language:* Bilingual (English/Urdu/French/Malay with functional to fluent classical Arabic).
  - *Geography:* Global diaspora (US, UK, Canada, Australia, Western Europe) and independent urban community mosques worldwide.
- **Why This User Matters:** The Friday khutbah reaches hundreds of millions of Muslims weekly. It is the single most influential, recurring live educational touchpoint in the Muslim world. The pulpit carries sacred authority; conveying inaccurate religious texts undermines communal trust and violates Prophetic guidance regarding narrating without verification.
- **User Context & Trigger:** Occurs every week between Tuesday evening and Friday morning. Triggered by the necessity of drafting a coherent, spiritually impactful 20–30 minute sermon on a specific contemporary or ethical theme.
- **Environmental Constraints:** Severe time pressure (often only 2–4 hours available late Thursday evening around a full-time job); working across multiple browser tabs on laptops or mobile devices; varying levels of access to expensive physical Arabic multi-volume hadith encyclopedias.
- **Existing Tools Used:** Dorar.net, Sunnah.com, Al-Maktaba Al-Shamela, Google Search, physical books (e.g., *Riyad al-Salihin*, *Mishkat al-Masabih*), WhatsApp peer groups, Apple Notes / Google Docs.

## 3. Secondary Users & Stakeholders
- **Secondary Users:** Mosque congregants (adults, youth, elders) who absorb, act upon, and share sermon takeaways with family and on social media; community elders who scrutinize preacher accuracy.
- **Human Reviewers / Authorities Involved:** Mosque Board of Directors / Religious Affairs Committees; senior regional scholars; local council of imams.

## 4. Job to Be Done
- **When:** Preparing a weekly Friday khutbah or public halaqah under a tight deadline.
- **I want to:** Quickly verify whether a remembered narration or story is authentically established (*sahih* / *hasan*), trace its original Arabic text (*matn*), identify who authenticated it across major hadith masters, and check its situational context (*Sabab al-Wurud*).
- **So that:** I can deliver an inspiring sermon with unassailable scholarly integrity without spending 6 hours manually cross-referencing disjointed websites or risking the recitation of an inauthentic report from the minbar.
- **Success looks like:** An imam can paste a remembered English or Arabic phrase and immediately receive a verified citation bundle (exact Arabic matn, primary source collection, hadith number, isnad grading consensus, circumstance of narration, and reputable translation) ready to drop into their sermon notes with total confidence.

## 5. Current Reconstructed Workflow
- **Trigger:** Wednesday evening: Imam selects sermon topic (e.g., "Compassion toward workers and subordinates").
- **Step 1: Ideation & Recall:** Imam recalls 2–3 relevant hadiths or stories from memory, old notes, or an online article (e.g., a famous story about the Prophet's servant Anas or a specific warning narration).
- **Step 2: Digital Search & Takhrij:** Imam opens browser. Searches English text on Sunnah.com. If not found or if grading is missing/ambiguous outside Sahihayn, imam copies the approximate Arabic text into Dorar.net (Al-Mawsu'ah al-Hadithiyyah).
- **Step 3: Discrepancy & Analysis Friction:** Dorar.net returns 25 different narrations and chains with diverging gradings from Al-Tirmidhi, Ibn Hajar, Al-Dhahabi, and Al-Albani (some say *hasan*, some say *da'if*, some grade a specific chain weak while another path is sound). The imam must manually parse complex *Mustalah* terminology in classical Arabic to determine if the narration can be used or if an authentic alternative exists.
- **Step 4: Context & Sharh Lookup:** To ensure the hadith isn't misinterpreted out of context, the imam searches Shamela or Google for classical commentary (*Fath al-Bari* or *Sharh al-Nawawi*) to confirm the *Sabab al-Wurud*.
- **Final Outcome:** After 4–6 hours across 8+ browser tabs, the imam finishes a draft sermon. If time runs out, the imam either cuts the narration out completely or takes the dangerous shortcut of reciting it unverified.

## 7. Core Pain Point
- **Exact Friction Point:** Step 3 & Step 4: The chasm between raw text search and synthesized scholarly authentication. Current digital tools either give English translations without deep takhrij (Sunnah.com) or dump raw, unranked, conflicting multi-century Arabic isnad evaluations without consensus synthesis (Dorar.net), lacking inline contextual commentary (*Sabab al-Wurud*).
- **Root Cause Hypothesis:** Hadith science (*'Ilm al-Hadith*) is inherently multi-dimensional (linking chains, narrator biographies, hidden defects, and contextual circumstances). Digital tools have simply digitized paper databases into keyword-searchable indices without building intelligent epistemic synthesis layers that resolve cross-scholar gradings for practicing educators.

## 8. Consequences & Impact
- **Immediate Consequence:** 4 to 8 hours wasted weekly per preacher on mechanical tab-switching; late-night cognitive exhaustion; rushed sermon delivery.
- **Long-term Consequence:** Preacher burnout; stagnation of khutbah topics (sticking only to the small subset of 30 universally known hadiths to avoid verification burdens); loss of intellectual depth in weekly community discourses.
- **Religious & Trust Consequence:** Public dissemination of fabricated (*mawdu'*) or severely weak (*da'if jiddan*) narrations from the pulpit; spiritual disillusionment when youth or congregants later discover a celebrated sermon story was untrue; violation of the Prophetic injunction against attributing unverified speech to the Messenger ﷺ.
- **Emotional / Cognitive Cost:** High anxiety, imposter syndrome, and vulnerability to hostile public challenges on social media or in mosque corridors.

## 10. Existing Workarounds
1. **Workaround 1 (The Dorar + Sunnah Tab-Juggling):** Keeping 6–10 browser tabs open (Sunnah.com, Dorar.net, Shamela, Google Translate, Google Docs) and manually copy-pasting text, isnads, and translations.
2. **Workaround 2 (Informal WhatsApp Scholar Chat):** Texting a knowledgeable colleague or seminary classmate late Thursday night asking: *"Is this hadith authentic?"*
3. **Workaround 3 (Pre-packaged Khutbah Repositories):** Downloading unvetted, pre-written sermon PDF scripts from online portals (e.g., KhutbahBank, IslamicCity) and reading them verbatim.
4. **Workaround 4 (Curricular Avoidance):** Deliberately restricting all sermons strictly to the 40 Hadith of Nawawi or Sahih Bukhari to avoid ever needing to perform takhrij on unfamiliar narrations.
- **Why Workarounds Fail:** Tab-juggling is exhausting and error-prone; WhatsApp contacts are busy and asleep at 11 PM; pre-packaged scripts are generic, dry, and culturally disconnected from local congregation needs; curricular avoidance starves the community of the vast breadth of the Prophetic tradition.

## 24. AI Opportunity Evaluation (Agent 06 — Phase 4)
- **Assigned AI Opportunity ID:** AI-OP-007 (Thematic Semantic Takhrij & Sabab al-Wurud Synthesis Workbench for Khatibs)
- **Strongest Non-AI Baseline:**
  - Multi-panel sermon drafting CMS integrating direct API / database connections to Sunnah.com and Dorar.net.
  - Relational concordance linking hadith canonical numbers to classical commentaries (*Fath al-Bari*, *Sharh Sahih Muslim*) and digitized *Sabab al-Wurud* volumes (*Asbab Wurud al-Hadith* by Al-Suyuti / Ibn Hamzah al-Husayni).
  - Pre-curated thematic index of ~1,000 common Friday khutbah topics with static, pre-verified hadith citations.
- **Specific AI Capability Justified:**
  - **Thematic Cross-Lingual Concept-to-Hadith Retrieval**: Imams recall sermon themes conceptually or by moral lesson ("dealing with economic hardship with dignity", "reconciling between estranged brothers", "Prophet's interaction with non-Muslim neighbors"). Exact keyword search on Sunnah.com / Dorar fails when specific words differ. Dense cross-lingual semantic search maps English/Arabic conceptual sermon prompts directly to the full Arabic hadith corpus.
  - **Extractive Sabab al-Wurud & Classical Commentary Chunking**: Classical explanations of the historical occasion of narration (*Sabab al-Wurud*) are embedded within multi-volume Arabic commentaries. A neural passage extractor isolates the specific historical context paragraph without generative alteration.
  - **Shepard's-Style Consensus Matrix (Isnad Treatment Summary)**: An aggregator that takes divergent scholar verdicts across centuries (e.g. Al-Tirmidhi, Ibn Hajar, Al-Albani, Shu'ayb al-Arna'ut) and surfaces a structured treatment matrix with warning badges if any major authority flagged severe defect (*'Illah*) or fabrication.
  - **Strict Architectural Boundary**: The AI is an *extractive research assistant*, NOT a sermon author. Generative AI drafting of sermons is strictly excluded to prevent theological superficiality and pulpit hallucination.
- **Why AI May Help:**
  - Compresses 2.5–4 hours of high-stress Thursday night research across 8 browser tabs into a 5-minute verified retrieval workflow.
  - Protects the pulpit (*minbar*) from the accidental dissemination of weak (*da'if jiddan*) or fabricated (*mawdu'*) hadiths while enabling imams to explore rich, less familiar authentic traditions beyond the 40 Hadith Nawawi.
- **Why AI May Be Unnecessary or Inferior:**
  - Generative text production (e.g. asking an LLM to "write a khutbah outline with hadiths") routinely invents phantom citations or presents weak folklore as Sahih. Generative LLMs must be barred from sacred text quotation without primary-key database constraints.
- **Expected Measurable Improvement:**
  - Weekly Sermon Preparation Time: Reduced from ~3 hours to <30 minutes.
  - Pulpit Citation Accuracy: Near-zero rate of unverified or fabricated hadiths cited in diaspora Friday sermons.
  - Thematic Variety: Khatibs report quoting from a 3x broader spectrum of authentic hadith collections (Musnad Ahmad, Bayhaqi, Tahawi).
- **Technical Feasibility & Data Constraints:**
  - *Data Availability:* High. Classical *Sabab al-Wurud* texts (Al-Suyuti, Ibn Hamzah) and classical commentaries are digitized in Shamela. Dorar.net provides comprehensive scholar grading records.
  - *Feasibility:* High. Prototype can be implemented as a desktop web editor with an embedded vector search sidebar.

## 25. Adversarial Red Team Review (Agent 07 — Phase 5)
- **Assigned Challenge ID:** RT-P-200 (The "Shepard's" Epistemic Fallacy & Sabab al-Wurud Scarcity)
- **Critical Assumptions Challenged:**
  1. *Citator Analogy Assumption:* Assumes Anglo-American legal citator systems (Shepard's / KeyCite) can be ported to Hadith sciences (*'Ilm al-Hadith*). Legal citators work because a hierarchical appellate court system formally overrules precedents; Hadith sciences possess no single judicial supreme court, only multi-generational scholarly *Ikhtilaf*.
  2. *Data Availability Assumption:* Assumes classical *Sabab al-Wurud* (occasions of narration) exists for the majority of thematic hadiths khatibs search for.
  3. *Workflow Adoption Assumption:* Assumes imams and volunteer khatibs will abandon Microsoft Word / Google Docs and physical notes to draft sermons in a specialized proprietary CMS.
- **P0 Failure Modes (Fatal Flaws):**
  - None that invalidate khatib verification anxiety, but fundamental epistemic limitations on the citator feature.
- **P1 Issues (Severe Complications):**
  - **P1-1 (The Epistemic Illegitimacy of an Automated Hadith "Treatment Badge"):** Porting Shepard's red/yellow/green warning flags to hadith authenticity is methodologically flawed. Al-Tirmidhi may grade a narration *Hasan-Gharib*, Ibn al-Jawzi may declare it fabricated (*Mawdu'*), Ibn Hajar may defend it as having corroborating chains (*Shawahid*), and Al-Albani may authenticate it. Forcing these nuanced evaluative debates into an algorithmic "consensus badge" requires the software to arbitrarily pick a "winning" methodology (e.g. defaulting to modern Salafi gradings vs classical late-Shafi'i/Hanafi assessments), instantly alienating rival theological traditions.
  - **P1-2 (Sabab al-Wurud Data Scarcity & Extraction Hallucination):** Fewer than 10% of prophetic hadiths have a documented, canonical *Sabab al-Wurud*. The primary reference works (Al-Suyuti, Ibn Hamzah) contain fewer than 1,500 reports combined. Applying neural passage extraction to general commentaries (*Fath al-Bari*) will inevitably mistake incidental juristic anecdotes or unrelated biographical details for genuine occasions of narration, misinforming the khatib's sermon.
  - **P1-3 (Commercial and Community Viability):** Mosque imams and volunteer khatibs operate under severe resource constraints. Volunteer khatibs preach once a month and rely on pre-existing notes; full-time imams have fixed sermon routines. Willingness to pay for specialized sermon drafting software is near-zero.
- **Theological & Ethical Risks:**
  - *False Historical Framing from the Minbar:* Fabricating or misattributing historical contexts (*Sabab al-Wurud*) during Friday congregational prayers compromises the sanctity of the pulpit.
  - *Distortion of Hadith Pluralism:* Imposing a mechanical citator over organic scholarly isnad criticism flattens classical hadith sciences.

## 26. Cross-Examination & Surviving Claims (Phase 6)
- **CX Reference ID:** CX-P-200 (Adjudicated under CX-DOCK-05 from RT-P-200)
- **Key Objections Debated:**
  1. *The Epistemic Citator Fallacy (P1):* Porting Shepard's / KeyCite legal citators to Hadith sciences (*'Ilm al-Hadith*) is an epistemic category error. Anglo-American law has a hierarchical appellate court system that formally overrules precedent; Hadith sciences possess no single judicial supreme court, only multi-generational scholarly evaluation (*Ijtihad*). An automated red/yellow/green "treatment badge" forces the software to pick a sectarian winner (e.g. modern Salafi vs. classical Shafi'i evaluation), falsifying scholarly pluralism.
  2. *Sabab al-Wurud Data Scarcity & Extraction Hallucination (P1):* Fewer than 10% of prophetic hadiths have a documented canonical *Sabab al-Wurud* (<1,500 reports combined in Suyuti and Ibn Hamzah). Applying neural passage extraction over general commentaries (*Fath al-Bari*) risks mistaking incidental anecdotes or juristic hypotheticals for canonical occasions of revelation, compromising the minbar.
  3. *Workflow Inertia & Micro-SaaS Infeasibility (P1):* Volunteer khatibs and imams have entrenched drafting habits in Microsoft Word, Google Docs, or physical notebooks; building a full standalone CMS has high switching friction and zero monetization potential.
- **Accepted Limitations & Scoped Boundaries:**
  1. *Mechanical Shepard's Treatment Badges Abandoned:* Algorithmic red/yellow/green consensus badges are **STRIPPED AND BARRED**. Replaced with an **Unweighted Comparative Scholarly Matrix** showing individual scholar evaluations with dates, book titles, and specific reasoning (e.g. Al-Tirmidhi Hasan, Ibn Hajar corroborated, Al-Albani Da'if).
